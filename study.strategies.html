Study Strategies

Active Recall

Active recall means forcing your brain to retrieve information from memory rather than passively rereading notes.To practice active recall, read a short section of your study material, close the book or hide your screen, and try to write down or speak the key concepts in your own words. Once you finish your attempt, open your source material to check for any mistakes or missing pieces, which highlights the exact areas that need more of your focus.

Spaced Repetition

Spaced repetition is a study method where you review new information at gradually increasing time gaps rather than cramming.To do this effectively, learn a topic, test your memory after 1 day, then 3 days, then a week, and then longer intervals, making the gaps wider each time you successfully remember the material.

Feynman Technique

The Feynman Technique is a four-step learning method that involves choosing a topic, teaching it simply, finding knowledge gaps, and refining your explanation

Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a simple time management method that uses a timer to split work into focused intervals separated by short breaks. The core steps involve choosing a task, setting a 25-minute timer, and taking a 5-minute break.

Interleaving

Interleaving is a study method where you mix related topics or skills during a single session rather than focusing on just one at a time. To do it effectively, choose 2 to 3 related subjects, mix different problem types, and alternate between them every 20 to 50 minutes.

Blurting

Blurting is an active revision technique where you write down everything you remember about a topic from memory, compare it to your notes, and correct your mistakes. The core steps involve choosing a specific topic, setting a timer, and writing freely on paper.

Targeted Review

A targeted review (such as a targeted literature review) means focusing on a specific goal, clear rules, and narrow search terms to save time. The main steps are: define your precise question, set strict inclusion limits, and search focused databases.
